Superior Court of the District of Columbia, Civil Division; D.C. Court of Appeals

Tax Analysts, et al. v. District of Columbia, et al.

Case No. 2020 CA 001999 B · Appeal No. 21-CV-0031 · FOIA litigation for disclosure of Office of Tax and Revenue private letter rulings
← Back to portfolio

Case Summary

In October 2019, Tax Analysts and reporter Aaron Davis filed a D.C. Freedom of Information Act request seeking 24 private letter rulings issued by the Office of Tax and Revenue between 2016 and 2019. The District refused, claiming the rulings were entirely protected by the tax-secrecy statute, D.C. Code § 47-4406.

Result

On July 27, 2023, the D.C. Court of Appeals reversed the Superior Court’s grant of summary judgment to the District and remanded the case. The appellate court held that private letter rulings are not categorically exempt: they may contain non-taxpayer-specific legal analysis that must be disclosed if reasonably segregable from protected tax information.

Why it matters

The decision rejected the District’s claim that OTR legal interpretations are “secret law.” It aligned D.C. with the federal IRS and most state revenue departments, which publish private letter rulings with identifying details redacted. On remand, the Superior Court was directed to conduct an in camera review to determine what portions of the 24 rulings could be released.
